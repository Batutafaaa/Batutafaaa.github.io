I am a Research Assistant at the Foundation for Agrarian Studies, where I work on the **Digital Agriculture in India** project. My current research work focuses on the agricultural drone ecosystem and mapping its regulatory architecture, analyzing its market dynamics, and identifying the public and private actors shaping this emerging sector.

My broader intellectual commitments lie at the intersection of political economy, state theory, and development. I am interested in how capitalist transformations reconfigure employment, governance, and environmental outcomes in India. This interest was developed by engagements with the work of Nicos Poulantzas, Bob Jessop, and Jairus Banaji, whose frameworks continue to shape my understanding of the state and its role in market formation.

Alongside this, I am developing competencies in quantitative economic analysis, with a particular interest in linear and nonlinear programming for optimal resource allocation. Inspired by the work of Leonid Kantorovich, A.R.G. Heesterman, Oskar Lange, and Paul Cockshott, I am exploring how these techniques might be mobilized to envision alternative governance models for socio-economic development.
